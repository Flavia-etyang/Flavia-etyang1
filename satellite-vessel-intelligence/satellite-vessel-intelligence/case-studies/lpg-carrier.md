## Inference

Cargo-containment architecture may indicate a gas-carrier configuration.
This is consistent with — but does not by itself confirm — an LPG
classification, since containment geometry differs by vessel design.

## Containment geometry note

LPG and LNG carriers are **not visually interchangeable** with each
other, and different LPG carriers are not visually interchangeable with
one another. Containment arrangements include:

- **Cylindrical tanks** — pressurized, semi-pressurized, or
  fully-refrigerated designs, often lower-profile
- **Spherical tanks** — Moss-type tank domes, a visually distinctive
  configuration illustrated in this case
- **Other containment arrangements** — membrane-type systems that sit
  flush within the hull and present minimal above-deck signature

A classification hypothesis should specify which containment pattern was
observed, rather than treating "gas carrier" as a single visual template.

## Core analytical lesson

A clear cargo deck alone does not establish an oil-tanker classification
— and equally, deck-mounted containment structures alone do not
establish a gas-carrier classification without considering hull
proportions and superstructure placement together.
